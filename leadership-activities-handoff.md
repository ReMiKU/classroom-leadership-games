# Handoff: Leadership Class Activities

## Brief

**Decision-Making & Problem Solving**
- Strategic and model for leading change
- Develop and implement strategies to achieve organizational goals
- Strengthen decision-making and critical thinking under pressure

**Constraints**
- 5 groups × 5 people
- Held in a normal classroom, with groups staying seated at their own tables (no moving tables aside)
- Low cost and low prep: no materials to make, ideally just a slide and a whiteboard
- Need 2 activities

**Status:** The two front-runners are **Red or Blue** and **The Auction**, both described in full below. Time per activity and slides are still open (see the last section).

---

## Activity 1: Red or Blue

**Covers:** strategy toward a shared goal, trust, and deciding under pressure.
**Duration:** about 20–25 minutes.
**Needs:** scrap paper for each group, a whiteboard for scores, the scoring table on a slide, and a timer.

### How a round works
1. Each group gets 30 seconds to secretly write **R** or **B** on a piece of paper.
2. On the facilitator's count, all five groups hold up their paper at once.
3. The facilitator records each group's points on the whiteboard using this table:

| Groups that chose Red | Each Red group gets | Each Blue group gets |
|---|---|---|
| 0 (all Blue) | – | +3 |
| 1 | +4 | −1 |
| 2 | +3 | −2 |
| 3 | +2 | −3 |
| 4 | +1 | −4 |
| 5 (all Red) | −2 | – |

Pattern: Red earns +1 for each group that chose Blue, and Blue loses 1 for each group that chose Red, with special scores when all five groups match.

### Structure
- Play **8 rounds**. **Round 4 counts ×2, round 6 counts ×3, and round 8 counts ×5.**
- Groups may not talk to each other, except for **one minute of negotiation before each bonus round** (4, 6 and 8). One spokesperson per group speaks from their seat.
- The only instruction given is **"score as many points as you can."** Do not clarify whether "you" means the group or the whole class.

### What usually happens
- Red always scores more for a single group, so someone picks it early and trust collapses.
- In the negotiation minutes, groups promise to all choose Blue, and someone often breaks the promise in the ×5 round.
- **Reveal at the end:** if every group had chosen Blue all 8 rounds, each would score **45** and the class would score **225**. Compare this with the actual class total.

### Debrief questions
- Did you take "you" to mean your group or the whole class?
- What happened after the first group picked Red?
- Who tried to get everyone to switch to Blue, and why did it work or fail?
- Where do departments in a real company behave like this?

## Activity 2: The Auction

**Covers:** developing and carrying out a strategy, adapting to change, and deciding under pressure.
**Duration:** about 20–25 minutes.
**Needs:** a whiteboard to track items and credits, a slide with the 12 items and forecast hints, a hidden slide with the event reveals, and a timer.

### Setup
- Each group is a startup team with **100 credits**. Their goal is to build the company most likely to survive its first year.
- Show all 12 items and three "market forecast" hints, then give **3 minutes** to plan what to chase and how much to pay.
- Example hints:
  - "Customers in this industry complain loudly online"
  - "The government is reviewing data protection rules"
  - "Competitors are poaching talent"

### Items (12)
Cybersecurity audit, Senior developer, Legal advisor, Compliance software, Customer support team, PR agency, Staff benefits package, Training programme, Cash reserve, Marketing campaign, Luxury office, Company cars.

### Bidding
- Sell items one at a time. Bidding starts at **5 credits** and rises in steps of **5**. The highest bid wins on "going once, going twice, sold".
- Only **one bidder per group** may bid, and that person **changes every three items**.
- **After the 6th item**, announce a news flash: *"Rumours of a price war next quarter."* This forces groups to rethink their plan mid-auction.
- Track each group's items and remaining credits on the whiteboard.

### Scoring (keep this secret until after the auction)
Reveal five events one at a time:

| Event | Items that protect you |
|---|---|
| Data breach attempt | Cybersecurity audit, Senior developer |
| New data protection law | Legal advisor, Compliance software |
| Viral customer complaint | Customer support team, PR agency |
| Competitor poaches your staff | Staff benefits package, Training programme |
| Price war | Cash reserve, Marketing campaign |

- A group with a protecting item gets **+20** for that event. A group without one gets **−10**.
- **Luxury office** and **Company cars** protect against nothing. They are there to tempt groups into wasting credits.
- Leftover credits are worth **1 point per 10 credits**.
- The highest total wins.

### Debrief questions
- What was your plan, and did you stick to it?
- Which item did you overpay for, and why?
- How did the news flash change your bidding?
- Who decided your maximum bid, and how?

## Other ideas considered (backups)

All of these also work with groups seated at their tables.

- **Crisis Room:** Each group is a company's management team. The facilitator reads a scenario (e.g. a food delivery startup facing a competitor's 30% price cut) and then announces "breaking news" every 3 minutes. Partway through, the "CEO resigns" and the person on their left takes over. It ends with a 60-second pitch from each group and a vote. Covers leading change.
- **Budget Cut:** Each group has RM100k and eight projects asking for RM250k in total. Each member secretly champions one department. Groups have 10 minutes to agree on what gets funded and defend it.
- **Lost at Sea:** Groups rank 15 survival items alone, then as a team, against the clock. The facilitator scores them against the expert ranking.
- **Quick-fire dilemmas:** The facilitator shows a dilemma with three options. Groups get 45 seconds to agree and answer with raised fingers. The leader rotates each round.
- **Spaceteam:** A free app for iPhone and Android, for 2 to 8 players. It is more about communication under pressure than strategy.
- **Bomb Squad:** The original idea, scaled so one defuser works with four experts who each hold part of the manual. Dropped because it needs a bomb and manual to be made.
