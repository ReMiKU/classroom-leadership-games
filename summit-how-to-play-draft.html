THE WHOLE GAME

Talk. Choose secretly. Reveal. Update Prestige. Repeat.

01 /

Your country. Your decision.

Your group leads a fictional country. Choose its name and a spokesperson. Everyone starts with 10 Prestige.

Play six rounds. The country with the highest Prestige at the end wins.

No money to manage. No army to move. No elimination. Prestige is the only score; even a country below zero stays in the game.

The islands are identities, not territories to conquer. Distance, borders and island size have no effect. You can trade with or target any other country.

02 /

Talk first. Commit together.

  1. 1

    Negotiate 90 SECONDS

    Make offers, promise a trade, or bluff. Stay seated: one spokesperson speaks for your country, while your group helps decide.

  2. 2

    Commit 30 SECONDS

    Write one action and its target, if needed. Submit it face down. No changes after submission.

  3. 3

    Reveal & resolve

    Once everyone has committed, the facilitator reveals each action. Apply the results together. Revealing later gives no advantage.

Rotate the spokesperson each round. The whole group still makes the decision.

03 /

Four actions. Pick just one.

ActionTargetWhat happens
01DevelopNoneGain +2 Prestige. You can still be hit by espionage.
02TradeOne other countryIf you both choose Trade with each other, each gains +3. Otherwise your trade earns 0.
03EspionageOne other countryIf they did not Defend, you gain +1 and they receive an espionage penalty of −2. If they Defended, your operation earns 0 and does no damage.
04DefendNoneGain +1 and block all espionage against your country this round.

One means one. No combining actions. No targeting yourself. Trade needs a mutual choice, not just a spoken promise.

A limit on damage: a country loses at most 2 Prestige from espionage per round, even if several countries target it. Every unblocked attacker still earns its own +1. These are rewards and penalties, not a fixed pool of transferred points.

Combine your result with any penalty. Develop +2 and espionage −2 means a net change of 0. Mutual Trade +3 and espionage −2 means a net change of +1.

The smaller, safer choice.

Defending earns less than developing or a successful trade. But if you expect to be targeted, it protects your country.

Espionage stays abstract: a dossier and a score effect. No separate riot system, war rules or real-world tactics to learn.

What if a promise breaks?

If Brume had secretly chosen Develop instead, Aster’s trade would earn 0 and Brume would gain 2. Neither receives the trade bonus.

What if everyone piles on?

Several countries may target Estra, but its espionage penalty is still only −2 in total. Each unblocked attacker receives +1. Defend blocks them all.

05 /

One country takes the title.

After six rounds,
highest Prestige wins.

Tied for first? Each tied country sends one champion for an in-person rock–paper–scissors knockout.

Random pairings, byes when needed, draws replayed. Continue until one country wins. The tiebreak changes the title, not the Prestige scores.

DESIGN /

A map, not a second rulebook.

The archipelago is my suggested direction: country identity, a readable score and one highlighted interaction. Everyone keeps their island. No movement, conquest, terrain advantages or range checks.

Alternative A · Country cards

Closest to Red / Blue. The clearest, lowest-effort board: flag, name, score, action. Less atmosphere, more space for large text.

Alternative B · The diplomatic table

Countries sit around a shared table. More negotiation atmosphere, less geographical. The same game underneath.

FOR YOU /

Facilitator notes.

Keep these behind the player guide in the eventual app.

Setup, slips & missed deadlines
  • Suggested first trial: five or six countries, roughly five students per group. Use fictional names and identical starting conditions.
  • Show the action table. Run one practice round. A scrap-paper slip is enough: write the country, action and target.
  • Collect every choice face down before revealing any. Check for missing targets or self-targets privately before the deadline.
  • If no valid action is submitted in time, default to Develop. Announce this rule before play. Never let a group correct its action after seeing another country’s choice.
  • Promises are not binding. Countries can bluff, but may not alter submitted choices. Rotate the spokesperson.
Exactly how to calculate the round
  1. Give Develop +2 and Defend +1.
  2. Give +3 to each country in a reciprocal Trade pair. Other Trade choices earn 0.
  3. Each Espionage action against a defending country is blocked. Every other attacker earns +1.
  4. Apply one −2 penalty to each undefended country targeted by one or more operations.
  5. Add all changes together per country, update Prestige, and show the log.

Work from all submitted actions, not from the reveal order. Trade can succeed while either partner is attacked; apply the espionage penalty separately. A Defend action is not also a Trade action.

Time budget & the classroom discussion

Target 35–45 minutes for a first trial. A suggested budget is 6 minutes explaining, 4 practising, 21 across six rounds, and 7 discussing: about 38 minutes before longer tiebreaks or delays. This is an estimate, not a playtest result.

If the hour must include Red / Blue too, try four Summit rounds and reserve time for the other game. Set the round limit before starting.

  • Which promise did you trust, and why?
  • Did you help your country or mainly hurt a rival?
  • When was a smaller, safer gain worth taking?
  • How did the group decide when members disagreed?
What this draft leaves out—and what needs testing

This is slightly more involved than Red / Blue, not equally simple. The extra choices are negotiation and sometimes a target. There is still only one score and one repeated round structure.

No world events, separate intelligence mission, stability track, army, economy or country-specific abilities yet. Secret intelligence would require another rule and a private delivery mechanism.

The scoring is proposed, not playtested. Check whether persistent trading pairs are too safe, whether Defend is attractive enough, and whether the espionage damage cap stays intuitive. Tune after a short trial, before building more systems.
